Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 August 2026

Outdo is a fitness competition app. It reads your Apple Watch Activity rings and compares them with your friends'. This page explains exactly what that means for your data — in particular the part people don't expect, which is how much of it your competitors can see.

The short version. Outdo reads three things from Apple Health: your Move, Exercise and Stand rings, plus the goals you set for them. When you join a competition, every other member of that competition sees your exact numbers. Your health data is never sold, never shared with advertisers, and never used to target advertising at you. You can delete all of it from inside the app, permanently, at any time.

Who is responsible for your data

Outdo is developed and operated by Arihant Gupta, an independent developer. For any question about this policy or your data, write to support.outdoapp@gmail.com.

What Outdo reads from Apple Health

With your permission, and only with your permission, Outdo reads:

  • Active energy burned — your Move ring, in kilocalories.
  • Exercise minutes — your Exercise ring.
  • Stand hours — your Stand ring.
  • The goals those rings were measured against — the Move, Exercise and Stand targets set on your Apple Watch. Outdo stores the goal alongside each day's numbers, because a score only means something next to the target it was measured against.

Outdo reads daily totals. It does not read workouts, heart rate, sleep, body measurements, clinical records, or any other health data type. Outdo never writes anything to Apple Health, and it cannot change your Apple Watch goals — those are yours to set in the Fitness app.

You can withdraw access at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Outdo. Outdo will stop reading new data immediately; anything already synced stays until you delete your account.

Health data Outdo does not read

Outdo reads the three Activity rings above and nothing else. It does not read your sleep, heart rate, heart-rate variability, workouts, body measurements, menstrual or reproductive health, clinical records, or any other Health category.

Features under consideration for a future Pro tier — such as sleep, recovery and heart-rate variability analysis — would need some of that data. None of it is read today, and none of it leaves your device today. If that ever changes:

  • Apple Health will ask your permission separately, per data type, on its own screen. Outdo cannot read a new category without you granting it there.
  • This page will be updated to describe it before the feature ships, and the date at the top will change.
  • The App Store privacy labels for Outdo will be updated to match.

Granting a new permission later is entirely your choice, and declining it only turns off the feature that needs it.

What else is stored

  • Your Apple account identifier. Outdo uses Sign in with Apple only. Apple gives the app an identifier unique to Outdo — never your Apple ID password, and never an identifier that other developers can match to you.
  • An email address. Apple provides one at sign-in, which is either your real address or a private @privaterelay.appleid.com forwarding address if you chose Hide My Email. Outdo stores it but does not currently display it, use it, or send mail to it.
  • Your display name and username. Your name is taken from your Apple account and can be changed in Settings. Your username is chosen once and is permanent.
  • Your daily scores. One record per day per competition: your ring totals, the goals they were measured against, and the points calculated from them.
  • Your competitions. Which ones you belong to, when you joined, your results in finished rounds, and nudges you have sent or received.
  • A notification token, if — and only if — you allow notifications. It lets a nudge from a friend reach your phone, and nothing else.

What other people can see

This is the important part, and it is deliberate. Outdo exists because capped, blurred fitness competition is boring and easy to game. Everyone in a competition you join can see your exact figures: your Move, Exercise and Stand numbers, the goals you were scored against, your points, your daily and best-day totals, your display name, your username, and how many rounds you have played in that group.

That transparency is the product. It is why nobody can quietly set a soft goal and win on paper. If you are not comfortable with a particular group seeing your numbers, do not join that competition — or leave it, which stops sharing your data with it from that moment.

People who are not in a competition with you cannot see your activity at all. Your personal, non-competition activity — what you see on the Home tab — is visible only to you.

Where your data lives

Outdo stores data in a PostgreSQL database hosted by Supabase, which processes it solely to provide the app. Push notifications are delivered through Apple's Push Notification service, and sign-in is handled by Apple.

Health data is never written to iCloud, which Apple's Developer Program License Agreement prohibits outright. Apple, Supabase and your own device are the only places your Outdo data exists.

What your health data is never used for

Plainly, and without qualification:

  • It is never sold, rented, or traded.
  • It is never used to target advertising or marketing at you — not by Outdo, not by anyone else. This covers your rings and goals and everything calculated from them: your points, your streaks and your competition results are all derived from your health data, so they are treated as health data too.
  • It is never shared with third parties beyond the infrastructure providers named above, who process it only to run the service.

This is not only our choice. Apple's rules for apps that read Apple Health prohibit using that data for advertising, marketing or use-based data mining, and prohibit disclosing it to third parties — and no amount of consent unlocks it.

The only place your health data is shared with other people is inside a competition you chose to join, as described above.

Advertising

Outdo shows no advertising today. We may add it in a future version — and if we do, the line above does not move: no advert will ever be selected or targeted using your health data, or anything derived from it. Not your rings, not your goals, not your points, streaks, rank or competition history.

In practice that means any advertising would be the same for everyone, or based only on information that has nothing to do with your health.

Before any advertising appears in Outdo, this page will be updated to describe it, and the App Store privacy labels for Outdo will be updated to match. If advertising ever involved tracking you across other companies' apps or websites, iOS would ask your permission first, on its own screen, and you could refuse.

Outdo does no advertising-network tracking today, which is why it does not currently ask for tracking permission.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and it goes, as described next.

Deleting your data

In the app: Home → your profile icon → Settings → Delete account. It is immediate and permanent — there is no grace period and no restore.

Deleting removes your profile, your username, every day of activity you have ever synced, your notification token, and your place on every leaderboard, including finished rounds you placed in.

One thing survives, on purpose: competitions you created keep running for the people still in them. A competition you started passes to whoever has been a member longest, so that other people's group does not disappear because you left. If you are the only member left, the competition is deleted along with your account.

Deleting your Outdo account does not touch your Apple Health data, which never leaves your iPhone's control and remains exactly as it was.

Children

Outdo is not directed to children under 13, and does not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has created an account, write to the address above and it will be removed.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or erase your personal data. Deletion is built into the app. For anything else, write to the address above and it will be handled.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects how your data is handled, the date at the top of this page changes and the change will be noted in the app's release notes. Continuing to use Outdo after a change means accepting the updated policy.
